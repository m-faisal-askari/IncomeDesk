# Brag Plan: IncomeDesk for Independent Filmmakers

## What is this app?
IncomeDesk is a financial operations workspace with project-level production financials: contract value, realized revenue, direct gear and crew costs, budget tracking, and take-home margin.

## The angle
Make the point from a filmmaker's world: the shoot can wrap while the production's financial picture is still fuzzy. IncomeDesk helps independent filmmakers connect a project's contract and payments to its direct costs, budget, and take-home margin. It is about the business behind a production, not video editing.

## Hook (first 2-3 seconds)
“The shoot wrapped. Did the project pay?” Open on a short-film slate marked WRAPPED, then cut to a sample production's real financial picture.

## Key moments (the middle)
- Show the IncomeDesk Project Financials view for the fictional short film *Afterlight*: $18,000 contract value, $18,000 realized revenue, $12,600 direct project costs, and $5,400 take-home profit (30% margin).
- Show a $13,500 production budget, $12,600 spent (about 93%), and $900 remaining.
- Make the cost records feel specific to production: camera and lens rental $4,800; contractor crew $5,200; sound and location permits $2,600. The rows sum to $12,600 and are linked to the project.

## Outro / punchline
“IncomeDesk. Keep production finances in frame.” Pair the wordmark with the actual Project Management & Production Financials screen.

## User flow worth showing
Create a client or independent project with a contract value and production budget → log direct costs such as gear rental and contractor crew to that project → review revenue realized, take-home profit, margin, and budget remaining.

## Tone
- Preset: polished
- Creative direction: a tidy “wrap report” for a small film production, with a clapperboard snap and calm, legible money cards
- Interpretation: use filmmaking cues as small, purposeful framing; keep the real project-financial fields as the hero and let the figures read before transitions move.

## Audio direction
- Role: restrained cinematic lift over a clear, professional bed
- Music: `happy-beats-business-moves-vol-12-by-ende-dot-app.mp3` by Sascha Ende / ende.app, CC BY 4.0
- Music treatment: steady bed at a restrained level; a light slate-like click on the WRAP moment, a soft accent on the profit reveal, and a gentle fade under the close.
- Music cue guidance: bundled Vol. 12 preset at about 109.96 BPM; target 8.74s for the take-home-profit reveal and 17.47s for the closing brand line. Use the beat grid for short cost-row arrivals only if the full breakdown has time to settle.
- Audio-reactive treatment: subtle breathing on the existing brand glow; no waveform or visualizer graphics.
- SFX posture: sparse and clean; use a small slate/click accent, one soft impact, and no continuous ticking.
- Audio-coupled moments: slate closes at the hook, project margin lands on the cue, budget progress settles as one complete visual.
- Restraint rule: filmmaking effects frame the financial work; they must not make IncomeDesk look like an editor, call-sheet app, or production-management suite.

## Storyboard

### Scene 1 — Wrap report — 5.0s
Hook: “The shoot wrapped. Did the project pay?” Show a clapperboard-style slate labeled `AFTERLIGHT · SHORT FILM`, `DEMO DATA`, and `WRAPPED`. The slate is a framing visual; it is not presented as an IncomeDesk feature.
Sequential/interaction: yes — a single clap-like close lands, then the project title and WRAPPED status hold.
Audio intent: one quiet click on the slate; music begins with a subtle cinematic lift.
Audio-coupled idea: snap the slate closed on the early beat and keep the hook visible.
Transition mood: confident cut → Scene 2

### Scene 2 — Project financials — 5.3s
Show the actual IncomeDesk project-financial fields for the fictional short film *Afterlight*: Contract Value $18,000; Revenue Realized $18,000; Direct Project Costs $12,600; Take-Home Profit $5,400; margin 30%. Label the figures as demo data. Use the exact product language “Project Financials” and “Take-Home Profit.”
Sequential/interaction: yes — project title appears first, then contract/revenue and cost/profit metrics arrive in paired groups and settle together.
Audio intent: one soft impact as take-home profit and margin resolve.
Audio-coupled idea: align the profit highlight to the 8.74s strong cue, but retain a readable hold.
Transition mood: smooth upward slide → Scene 3

### Scene 3 — Production budget — 5.3s
Show Production Budget $13,500, direct costs $12,600, 93% spent, and $900 remaining. List camera and lens rental $4,800, contractor crew $5,200, sound and location permits $2,600. Show these as costs linked to *Afterlight*. Keep the remaining-budget figure distinct from take-home profit.
Sequential/interaction: yes — the progress bar fills once; three cost rows appear quickly and hold together.
Audio intent: short, quiet accents only; let the completed budget view hold.
Audio-coupled idea: reveal the rows in a measured beat-grid sequence, then show the full cost list.
Transition mood: soft resolve → Scene 4

### Scene 4 — The business behind the film — 6.3s
Close on the IncomeDesk mark and project-financials language: “Contract value. Production costs. Take-home margin.” Final line: “Keep production finances in frame.” The product remains clearly positioned as finance management for independent filmmakers.
Sequential/interaction: yes — the three short field names arrive as one grouped lockup, then the closing line settles on the 17.47s cue.
Audio intent: one restrained brand accent and a gentle music fade.
Audio-coupled idea: land the closing line near 17.47s and hold the wordmark.
Transition mood: clean end card → end
