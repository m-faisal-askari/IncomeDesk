# Brag Plan: IncomeDesk

## What is this app?
IncomeDesk is a financial workspace for independent freelancers to track confirmed income, expenses, cash flow, and estimated tax reserves.

## The angle
A payment arrives, expenses follow, and the freelancer is left asking where the money went. The video starts in that uncertainty, then introduces IncomeDesk as the place to see income and costs together, understand the month's cash flow, and plan an estimated tax reserve.

## Hook (first 2-3 seconds)
“Money came in. Money went out.” Land the follow-up: “No idea where it went.” A messy but legible sample activity list makes the problem tangible.

## Key moments (the middle)
- Meet IncomeDesk as a freelancer finance workspace; show confirmed income and expenses together.
- Recreate the app's Financial Overview with sample income of $6,200, expenses of $2,860, and net cash flow of $3,340.
- Carry the sample cash-flow figure into a clearly labeled estimated tax reserve: 20% of $3,340 is $668.

## Outro / punchline
“IncomeDesk. Put freelance money in focus.” Keep the wordmark and the product's actual freelance finance UI visible together.

## User flow worth showing
Money arrives and expenses are recorded → the Financial Overview shows income, expenses, and net cash flow → the tax planning view displays a sample estimated reserve.

## Tone
- Preset: polished
- Creative direction: an empathetic “where did it go?” moment that resolves into calm financial clarity
- Interpretation: give the problem a readable hold, then make the product screens feel organized and reassuring through smooth reveals and restrained sound.

## Format: vertical — 1080x1920
## Duration: 21 seconds

## Visual identity (from the project)
- Background: `#000000` and `#0E0E11`
- Accent: `#35C98A`, with brand green `#08764A`
- Text: `#FFFFFF`, secondary `#D1D5DB`, muted `#9CA3AF`
- Display font: Inter
- Body font: Inter
- Strongest visual element: the Financial Overview showing Net Cash Received, Total Expenses, and Net Cash Flow.

## Share copy (draft)
Money came in, then went somewhere. IncomeDesk puts freelance income, expenses, cash flow, and tax estimates in one clear view.

## Audio direction
- Role: warm, polished bed that moves from uncertainty to clarity
- Music: `happy-beats-business-moves-vol-12-by-ende-dot-app.mp3` by Sascha Ende / ende.app, CC BY 4.0
- Music treatment: start immediately at a restrained level, then fade under the final brand frame; let the main product reveal and reserve amount land cleanly.
- Music cue guidance: bundled Vol. 12 preset at about 109.96 BPM; target strong cues at 8.74s for the Financial Overview reveal and 17.47s for the reserve amount. Use the beat grid for small UI arrivals only where reading time is preserved.
- Audio-reactive treatment: subtle; let the existing brand glow breathe with the music, with no waveform or visualizer.
- SFX posture: sparse and soft; a quiet activity-list tick, one product reveal accent, and one reserve confirmation cue.
- Audio-coupled moments: sample activity rows appear in sequence and then hold together; the Financial Overview settles on the strong cue; the estimated reserve amount lands on the later cue.
- Restraint rule: keep the hook readable and never make the financial figures feel frantic.

## Storyboard

### Scene 1 — The mystery — 5.0s
Headline: “Money came in. Money went out.” Follow-up: “No idea where it went.” A fictional recent-activity card lists a $6,200 client payment and three costs ($1,150 rent, $980 tools and software, $730 project expenses), without showing a clear total. Label the figures as demo data. The rows appear one by one, then remain visible long enough to read.
Sequential/interaction: yes — reveal the four activity rows quickly in order, then hold the full list.
Audio intent: a light, restrained tick as entries appear; keep the opening bed calm.
Audio-coupled idea: align only one or two row accents to the beat; preserve a readable hold.
Transition mood: clean horizontal wipe → Scene 2

### Scene 2 — Meet IncomeDesk — 5.2s
Show IncomeDesk and the actual Financial Overview language. Replace the loose activity list with a composed monthly card: confirmed income +$6,200 and tracked expenses $2,860, both marked as sample values. Copy: “Bring freelance income and expenses into one clear view.”
Sequential/interaction: yes — the income and expense lines settle into the same card, then hold together.
Audio intent: warm reveal accent; move from uncertainty to control.
Audio-coupled idea: the card arrives on the main reveal; keep the two readable lines on screen together.
Transition mood: smooth upward slide → Scene 3

### Scene 3 — See the month clearly — 5.9s
Recreate the app's Financial Overview: Net Cash Received $6,200; Total Expenses $2,860; Net Cash Flow $3,340. Copy: “See what came in, what went out, and what's left.” Mark as demo data. Reveal the three rows one by one, with the net cash-flow result receiving the strongest emphasis.
Sequential/interaction: yes — reveal three dashboard metrics in order; keep the full dashboard visible after the final row.
Audio intent: measured, confident pulse; a soft accent when net cash flow resolves.
Audio-coupled idea: the completed overview lands at the 8.74s strong cue; the three text rows keep a readable hold.
Transition mood: clean upward slide → Scene 4

### Scene 4 — Plan the reserve — 6.3s
Show Estimated Tax Reserve $668, Demo target 20%, Sample profit $3,340, and “Planning Estimates Only.” Then close on the IncomeDesk lockup and “Put freelance money in focus.” The numbers remain labeled as demo data and the reserve is clearly an estimate.
Sequential/interaction: yes — reveal the estimate after the card, then settle the wordmark and closing line.
Audio intent: one soft confirmation cue on the reserve amount, then a gentle musical fade.
Audio-coupled idea: land the $668 figure on the 17.47s cue and hold the final wordmark.
Transition mood: soft resolve → end

**Music mood for this video:** warm, polished, reassuring.
**Audio summary:** a light business beat carries the story from a scattered activity list to an ordered dashboard and a calm reserve plan.
